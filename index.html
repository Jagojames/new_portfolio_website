ECA International

Reimagining global
mobility with AI.

Exploring how AI could simplify the complex process of estimating the cost of relocating employees around the world.

// Context

The opportunity

ECA International provides data, software and advisory services for organisations managing global employee assignments — from cost-of-living and salary data to housing, taxation and expatriate allowances.

Relocation estimates are inherently complex. Costs vary by country, city, salary, housing, taxation, allowances and individual circumstances.

The opportunity was to explore whether AI could make that complexity easier to navigate — without losing the accuracy and expertise ECA's customers depend on.

// My role

Brought in to explore what was possible.

I was brought in as a contractor to work on an innovation project exploring how AI could transform the relocation estimation experience.

Working from an early-stage concept, I explored different interaction models, product opportunities and ways AI could change how people interact with ECA's complex data.

The work involved moving quickly from ideas to tangible prototypes — using design to explore, communicate and test what a future AI-powered experience could look like.

Three experiments.
One bigger question.

How might AI change the way people interact with complex global mobility data?

Rather than designing a single solution, I explored several possible directions for how AI could simplify the experience.

01

AI Transcribe & Build

From conversation to structured requirements.

AI Conversational UX

Exploring how unstructured information could be captured, interpreted and transformed into the building blocks of a relocation estimate. Rather than asking users to fill in structured forms, what if they could just describe the situation — and AI handled the interpretation?

02

Mobile AI

A more natural way to interact with relocation intelligence.

Mobile UX Conversational AI

Exploring how AI could create a more accessible and conversational experience for interacting with complex relocation information on mobile. Most existing tools are designed for desktop power users — this explored a different kind of entry point.

Prototype coming soon
03

AI Builder

Helping users build complex relocation estimates through AI.

AI Product Design

Exploring how AI could help users create, refine and understand relocation scenarios without having to navigate every underlying variable themselves. The goal was to make the full power of ECA's data accessible — without requiring expert knowledge to use it.

Prototype coming soon
// The design challenge

Making complexity feel simple.

The challenge wasn't simply to add an AI interface to an existing product.

It was to understand where AI could genuinely reduce complexity — and where human expertise, transparency and control still mattered.

Automation

Let AI carry the weight

Letting AI handle complexity where it could — so users don't have to think about every variable.

Control

Keep humans in charge

Ensuring users stayed in control of important decisions, rather than handing everything to the machine.

Clarity

Make the invisible visible

Making complex calculations and recommendations understandable — not a black box of AI output.

Trust

Build confidence, not dependency

Ensuring the experience didn't erode the expert trust ECA's customers already place in their data.

// Process

Using prototypes to make the future tangible.

Because the project was exploratory, the prototypes became a way of thinking. Rather than trying to define the entire product upfront, I used interaction design to explore different possibilities, expose assumptions and communicate ideas with stakeholders.

Idea → Interaction → Prototype → Evaluate → Refine

The goal was not to create a finished product. It was to discover what the product could become.

// What I took from it

AI is most useful when it removes complexity — not when it simply adds intelligence.

The best AI experience isn't necessarily the one with the most AI. It's the one that makes something previously difficult feel considerably easier.

For ECA, that meant exploring how AI could sit between the user and a highly complex body of data — helping people get to useful answers without needing to understand everything happening underneath.

backbone for cleaner shipping.

// Context

Reducing emissions at sea.

Silverstream Technologies provides air lubrication systems for ships — technology that reduces friction between a vessel's hull and the water, cutting fuel consumption and emissions significantly.

As the company scaled, they needed a digital layer to make their ALS technology visible, trackable and understandable — for a diverse audience of ship operators, marine engineers and executive teams.

There was no existing design system, no product precedent. Everything had to be built from scratch.

// My role

Head of Design on a greenfield product.

I led the design of a digital performance web application and iOS app for Silverstream's Air Lubrication System — working across a cross-functional team of data scientists, marine engineers and front-end developers.

The application displays live ALS performance metrics, enables vessel tracking and supports maintenance monitoring — all designed for a diverse range of shipping users with very different levels of technical familiarity.

In my first week, I also presented to the company's town hall — proposing an updated colour strategy to align the brand's visual identity with its environmental mission.

Four products.
One six-month sprint.

Platform hub, estimation tool, iOS app, and a full design case study — all delivered within the same window.

To manage scale without sacrificing quality, we built a centralised hub as the foundation — so each new application could plug in and benefit from the same architecture.

01

Silverstream Hub

A centralised platform to unify all ALS applications.

Platform Design MVP
Video

As we embarked on developing multiple applications for Silverstream, it became clear that we needed a robust and scalable solution to support future growth. To future-proof our efforts and streamline integration, we decided to build a centralised hub — a foundation for all applications, ensuring seamless connectivity, easier maintenance and a more cohesive user experience.

To validate this approach, we designed and built an MVP to test our initial applications within the hub, allowing us to assess effectiveness in real-world scenarios and gather crucial feedback before scaling further.

The Silverstream Hub MVP

02

Estimation Tool

Helping the sales team quantify ALS fuel savings in real time.

Data Viz Sales Tool
Video

I designed a sales estimation tool in collaboration with data scientists, marine engineers and front-end developers. The tool allows users to calculate potential fuel savings from the Silverstream ALS — by selecting ship type (cruiser, tanker, bulker) and inputting parameters such as Deadweight Tonnage, Length Between Perpendiculars, beam and ballast.

Results display as a power-over-speed chart across ballast, laden and scantling conditions — showing gross and net savings and total supplied power. Completed in approximately one month.

The estimation tool — used by sales to calculate ALS system savings

iOS App

Despite the challenge of designing wireframes for multiple applications concurrently, the iOS MVP was delivered on time — demonstrating the team's ability to manage complex parallel workstreams without sacrificing quality.

// The design challenge

Designing for a world most designers never see.

Shipping is a highly specialised, technical domain. Users range from ship captains checking operational performance to data scientists reviewing emission metrics to sales teams calculating ROI for prospects.

Designing for all of them — simultaneously, in a greenfield context, under a tight deadline — required a design approach that was systematic from day one.

Clarity

Data that speaks clearly

Making complex ALS performance metrics legible and actionable for non-technical users without removing depth for expert users.

Scalability

Build once, extend forever

Designing the Hub as a foundation meant every future application could inherit the same design language and infrastructure.

Diversity

One product, many users

Ship engineers, executives, data scientists and sales teams — each with different needs, mental models and levels of technical fluency.

Speed

Four MVPs in six months

The delivery timeline was aggressive. Parallel workstreams, rapid wireframing and close cross-functional collaboration kept everything on track.

// Process

Immerse, collaborate, iterate.

Designing for a highly specialised technical domain requires genuine immersion. Before drawing a single screen, I worked closely with marine engineers and data scientists to understand what the ALS system actually does — and what would make it meaningful on screen.

Immerse → Wireframe → Test → Build → Ship

Running parallel workstreams across hub, estimation tool and iOS required constant prioritisation — designing foundational patterns first, then letting each application inherit and extend them.

// What I took from it

Great design in unfamiliar territory starts with genuine curiosity.

Shipping is a world most designers never enter. Getting close to it — really close — was what made the work credible, useful and trusted by engineers who'd never worked with a designer before.

Building the Hub as a foundation also taught me the long-term value of investing in architecture early. Every hour spent on the platform saved multiples of that time across every product that followed.

// The Brief

Global Relay - a financial SaaS archiving and compliance platform - asked candidates to design a unified login panel handling two distinct authentication flows: traditional ID/password, and SAML-based SSO redirected to a customer-hosted page.

// Core requirements
01 · Determine Login Method

System detects whether a user is configured for password or SSO based on their Login ID - without users needing to know which they use.

02 · Password Auth

Standard ID/password flow with full error handling and security - malicious users must not be able to enumerate valid IDs.

03 · SSO / SAML Flow

After ID lookup, redirect to customer-hosted login page. Global Relay never sees the password. Handle all edge cases and errors.

04 · Scope

Responsive - large monitors to mobile browsers. Single-login UX (log in once, in to everything). Localisation-ready. Async error states.

View full brief PDF
// The Work
Design response - 30 pages
Competitor analysis · Personas · Flows · Wireframes · Error states · Accessibility
↗ Open in new tab
// Process highlights
Research

Competitor analysis across Miro, Amplitude, Zoom, Stripe, Adobe and Coinbase - studying login flow patterns and SSO handling.

Security

Reviewed NIST, OWASP, and SAML standards. Error messages deliberately vague to prevent ID/password enumeration attacks.

Flows

Happy path, SSO path, error states, password reset, MFA, and account lockout - all documented with annotated wireframes.

Accessibility

WCAG-aligned labels, keyboard navigation, screen reader support, and clear input validation with inline guidance.

// The Brief

Zen Educate is a two-sided marketplace connecting schools with educators for short and long-term work. The June 2026 challenge set a hypothetical Edinburgh scenario - the marketplace looks healthy on the surface, but five signals suggest educators aren't being served across their full lifecycle.

// Edinburgh scenario - five signals
35% · Post-placement drop-off

Educators who complete a long-term placement do not return to Zen for their next role.

50% · Early friction

Verified educators do not secure work within their first 10 days.

<1× · Engagement gap

Educators in long-term roles open the app less than once per week vs. 8+ times for daily supply staff.

4:1 · Marketplace imbalance

Supply to demand ratio creates a "Hunger Games" dynamic - fatigue and disengagement.

25% · Perceived utility ceiling

Respondents say they don't find Zen useful for anything beyond accepting work.

// The task - two parts
Part 1 · 30% - Discovery

Is there meaningful additional value to unlock? How would you structure discovery, prioritise signal, and decide whether this is a supply/demand constraint or a real product gap?

Part 2 · 70% - Design the system

Pick a cohort, define the problem, choose a direction, and bring it to life. End-to-end journey, key screens, behaviour change, and how you'd measure it. High enough fidelity to assess design quality.
